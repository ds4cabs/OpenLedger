# Why OpenLedger Matters

## The Problem
Pharma is one of the largest industries on Earth, yet the relationship between an issuer's filings (10-K, 10-Q), its product portfolio, and the actual FDA / Medicare landscape is opaque to anyone outside specialist analyst teams. A generalist investor reading Pfizer's 10-K cannot easily map *Eliquis* to its NDA, its label, its exclusivity expiry, or its Medicare ASP — yet those facts drive the asset's value.

## What OpenLedger Does
Cross-walks SEC filings, FDA Orange Book / Drugs@FDA, drug labels, and pricing into a unified issuer card. Investors can answer questions like "which of this company's revenue is at loss-of-exclusivity risk in 24 months?" in seconds rather than days.

## Who Benefits
- Generalist investors without sector-specialist teams
- Retail investors making decisions on pharma names
- Journalists and researchers tracking exclusivity dynamics
- BD teams running comparable-deal analyses

## Why Now
SEC EDGAR, FDA, and CMS data are all structured and queryable. LLMs are uniquely good at the name-normalization problem (matching *apixaban* to *Eliquis* to NDA 022512) that historically required brittle heuristic cross-walks.

## The Bigger Picture
Capital flows toward better-informed investors. When the public can analyze pharma assets at issuer level, capital allocation across the industry improves — and that, eventually, translates into more efficient funding of the drugs that actually matter.
